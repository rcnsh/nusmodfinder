# Plain-language review: reasons, no-match and error sentences

Red-team pass (5G) on every sentence an exchange student can see that says why a course is closed, what condition it carries, why a search found nothing, or that something failed. The reader assumed: an incoming exchange student reading English at B1–B2.

## Method

- Read every reason text in `data/ng-reasons.json` (130 ids, 117 distinct texts) and the generic fallback in `src/ng-reasons.js`.
- Read every template that rewords or wraps them in `public/explain.js` (`statusText`, `stampText`, `noteLead`, `noteText`, `reasonLine`, `sourceNote`, `filterMisses`, `hiddenCodeLine`, `hiddenCodeStamp`, `notOfferedLine`, `partialCodeLine`, `codesCount`, `unpinnedLine`). Rendered them with real reason values via `node` (importing `public/explain.js`, `public/messages.js` and `public/format.js`), so the sentences below are what a student sees after the template is filled.
- Read `public/messages.js` (`ERROR_SENTENCES`, `errorSentence` incl. the three Retry-After forms, `NO_MATCH`, `noMatchSentence`, `degradedNote`), and the empty, error, missing-field and key/gloss text in `public/app.js`, `public/render.js`, `public/detail.js`, `public/format.js` and `public/index.html`.
- Checked the claim in `public/messages.js` that the server's `message` is never shown. It holds: `public/api.js` keeps only `body.error` (`getJson`, lines 63–65), and every caller turns that code into a sentence through `errorSentence`; an unknown code falls back to the `http` sentence.

That makes **229 sentences or sentence templates reviewed**: 118 reason texts, 40 explain.js templates and stamps, 20 messages.js sentences, and 51 other visible empty, error, missing-field, key and gloss lines. **41 are flagged**: 8 at rank 1, 18 at rank 2 and 15 at rank 3. Where one sentence appears under several reason ids, it gets one row.

Ranks: **1**: could make a student decide wrongly about taking a course. **2**: leaves them unsure what it means or what to do. **3**: minor (wording, jargon or consistency that slows a reader down without misleading them).

Every rewrite keeps the source rule's meaning. Where a rewrite adds a next step ("ask NUS", "ask your exchange office"), it is advice, not a claim about the rule.

## Terms to explain once

These recur across many sentences. Explaining each once, in the sources box on the first screen (`renderSources`, `public/app.js:822`) and in the rail's key, does more than rewording every sentence. Once a term is explained there, the per-sentence rewrites above can use it bare.

- **NG / non-graduating**: NUS's word for exchange students. It is glossed already; keep it, and use "exchange students" in sentences.
- **NG list** and **NG restrictions list**: name both, once, with what each decides. The second is currently cited but never explained.
- **Registrar**: the NUS office that publishes both lists. After that, refer to the lists by name, not by the office.
- **NUSMods**: the timetable site the course facts come from (it is named in the footer but not explained).
- **Course level** (1000–6000): the first digit of the code; 5000 and above are graduate courses. Also say how "level 4000 and higher" is written.
- **How course codes are built**: the letters at the start name the subject (CG, DSA, LSM…), and a letter at the end (X, Y) marks a special version. This lets texts say "courses whose code starts with CG".
- **Undergraduate / graduate**: as NUS uses them, matched to the year-of-study choices.
- **Graded basis, audit, S/U**: "for a grade"; "attend without a grade"; NUS's pass/fail option (the "Can be taken S/U" chip in `public/intents.js:30` uses it bare).
- **Approval, appeal, case by case**: what each asks the student to do, and who to ask.
- **Quota / places**: a course may fill up.
- **Partner university / partner school**: a university with an agreement with that NUS school; ask your exchange office.
- **Special term**: NUS's short vacation terms; the NG list doesn't cover them.
- **Prerequisite** and **preclusion**: "a course you must pass first" and "a course you can't take if you've taken a similar one". "Preclusions" is a detail heading (`public/detail.js:80`) that most readers won't know.
- **Units**: NUS's credit measure (the filter and the entries say "units").
- **UROP** and **self-funded programme**: only if rows 30–31 keep the terms.
